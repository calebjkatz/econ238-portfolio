Public notice · A proposed cultural moat

Small Classes.
Nonnegotiable.

What should Rochester preserve? Guarantee every undergraduate access each semester to small, faculty-led discussions of no more than 15 students. Sessions would explore courses, research, and professional careers, sometimes inviting working professionals. Direct conversation would become an institutional promise, not an optional luxury.

What stands in the way? Rising costs, enrollment pressures, standardized online courses, and AI-driven instruction make larger classes attractive. This commitment would deliberately resist that pressure.

Who pays? Additional faculty time and guest coordination increase costs. Students might face higher tuition and faculty heavier workloads. The largest cost could be what Rochester gives up: research, financial aid, or other teaching initiatives.

Why accept the cost? Students could build relationships, explore research, and understand careers from people doing the work. But a cheaper annual seminar might achieve similar benefits, and extra spending could reduce access. Those alternatives deserve scrutiny.

What is the moat? It binds Rochester to sustained human teaching and pushes back against the idea that education should be endlessly scalable. Its value must outweigh the opportunities lost.

Would you pay more to guarantee meaningful conversations with faculty and working professionals throughout your Rochester education?

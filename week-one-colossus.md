# Data Centers and Water: How Significant Is 800,000 Gallons a Day?

*By Caleb Katz · Econ 238 · Week One*

The constant and aggressive expansion of data centers continues as yet another one is in development for construction. This newest expansion is predicted to consume 800,000 gallons of water per day. What does this mean for those nearby?

There are many large statistics about data centers. They are massive buildings that require massive amounts of resources. Of course they will have large statistics. The question that should be asked is not “how large are these statistics?” Rather the question that should be asked is “how significant are these statistics?” This new data center will be using 800,000 gallons of water per day, but what does that actually mean?

## Putting Water Use in Context

The water being used in data centers needs to be pure, or at least mostly pure, water. They can not use undrinkable salt water without purifying it first. The cheapest option for the owners of the data center will be to purchase the water from the city. Depending on the capacity of the city, this can range from a massive dent in the water supply, to a small dink. According to the [Monroe County Water Authority (MWCA)](https://www.mcwa.com/my-water/water-faqs/), the Shoremont Water Treatment Plant, which is the primary source of treated drinking water for the county outside the City of Rochester, can produce up to 140 million gallons of water per day. This would mean that a data center consuming this amount of water would account for less than one percent of what the county as a whole could produce. That sounds just like a mere dink in the supply for those in Monroe Country. The current rate at which water from the plant is used is on average around 50 million gallons per day. This means that the data center is totally feasible for the county.

## What Happens as Data Centers Expand?

What if the data centers keep expanding? There has been a rapid increase in development of data centers. Say two more in Monroe county itself start development and have a similar water need. Then data centers will be consuming two percent of the maximum production of water in the county, around six percent of an increase of what is already being consumed. This can get out of hand very quickly if the community doesn’t act. A county such as Monroe County is fortunate enough to have a water supply system that operates at such a high capacity, but not all counties are so lucky.

The water consumption isn’t the only concerning statistic about this new data center. Electrical use is always going to be a huge concern. These massive facilities consume an incomprehensible amount of power to run. Counties that have the electrical infrastructure for that will be ok, but those that do not will suffer just like those without enough of a water supply.

Data centers do provide value however. Our modern world is run on the internet. The internet only functions with data centers. Data centers have always existed, but now they are being built at a record rate. At this point, we as ordinary citizens could not easily live without data centers, so is it really so bad that yet another one is under construction?

## Evaluating Community Needs

What is the best solution? There isn’t really a best solution. Everyone has their own motivations and interest. Some value data centers more than others. Some are investing and data centers while others are attempting to destroy them. The best course of action is to make sure the developers of these data centers are being mindful of how it will impact the community they are entering, while those hosting data centers accurately evaluate their utilitarian needs before hosting one.

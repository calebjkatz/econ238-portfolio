About the lecture

At the turn of the twenty-first century, the world's appetite for electronics helped transform an obscure mineral into an extraordinary economic opportunity. In eastern Congo, coltan mining offered families new incomes while armed groups and political elites competed to control the trade. But the Congo wars began before the mineral boom, and the collapse in prices did not end the exploitation. This lecture explores how valuable resources can finance both livelihoods and violence, why prosperity depends on more than what lies underground, and how the institutions governing wealth can matter more than the wealth itself.

# ECON 238 — Homework 7: Drilling for Achievable Ideas

**Proposition:** Keep the Dams. Restore Salmon Habitat.

**Public proposition and voting site:** https://calebjkatz.github.io/econ238-portfolio/homework-7/

**Ballot site:** https://calebjkatz.github.io/econ238-portfolio/homework-7/ballot/

## Proposal in brief

Keep the four lower Snake River dams in operation while requiring the Bonneville Power Administration to fund an **additional $50 million per year in salmon habitat restoration for ten years** ($500 million total). The added expense would be recovered through wholesale electricity rates, and the work would undergo independent assessment. This is an imagined ballot proposal, not an existing law or a guarantee of salmon recovery.

## Most interesting voter condition

The only conditional voter proposed lowering the ten-year budget to **$100 million instead of $500 million**, arguing that the larger total was difficult to justify for salmon. This reveals a real tradeoff rather than simple unconditional support or opposition: agreement with an environmental goal does not automatically imply agreement with any proposed cost.

## Short reflection on the experience

The response pattern made me think differently about what makes an environmental bargain acceptable. Eleven of the twelve ballots accepted the deal as written, but one voter would support it only at a much smaller price. That shows why a proposal needs a specific budget and a way to judge what it buys; otherwise, people may agree with the goal without confronting the tradeoff. Cutting the proposed budget by 80% might make the idea politically easier to accept but could also buy less habitat restoration. The next economic question is what improvement in salmon outcomes each additional dollar would actually deliver. The poll is only a small, voluntary classroom sample, not evidence of broad public opinion or proof that this program would recover salmon.
